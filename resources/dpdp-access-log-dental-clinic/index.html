Does DPDP Require an Audit Log of Who Opened Patient Records?

Photo: Khrystinasnell, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons.

If you are comparing dental software, you have probably seen promises like a "DPDP-compliant audit trail" or "HIPAA-compliant servers". It is hard to tell which of these the law actually asks of a small clinic, and which are sales language.

The short answer is yes. India's data protection rules do ask a clinic to keep logs of who accesses patient data, and to keep those logs for a year. They do not name a product, a format or a feature called an audit trail. This article goes through the exact rule, what it means for a clinic with a few staff logins, and what to ask a software vendor.

The Rule That Asks for Logs

The rule is Rule 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, published in the Gazette on 13 November 2025. It says a The person or business that decides why and how personal data is used. For patient records, that is your clinic. must protect the personal data it holds with "reasonable security safeguards", and lists what those must include "at the minimum". Four of the seven items in that list matter here:

  • 6(1)(b): "appropriate measures to control access to the computer resources" used to hold the data.
  • 6(1)(c): "visibility on the accessing of such personal data, through appropriate logs, monitoring and review, for enabling detection of unauthorised access, its investigation and remediation to prevent recurrence".
  • 6(1)(d): reasonable measures to keep working if data is lost or damaged, "such as by way of data-backups".
  • 6(1)(e): "retain such logs and personal data for a period of one year, unless compliance with any law for the time being in force requires otherwise".

So the law asks for three things together: control over who can get in, a record of who did get in, and someone looking at that record often enough to notice a problem. A log that nobody ever reads does not meet the words "monitoring and review".

When It Starts

Rule 1(4) of the same notification says Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 come into force "eighteen months after the date of publication". Rule 6 is in that group, so it binds from May 2027, the same date as the other main obligations in our DPDP checklist for dental clinics. That leaves time to choose software that can produce the logs, but not so much that it can wait until the software contract renews.

What Counts as a Log

Rule 6 does not define one. Read with its stated purpose, "detection of unauthorised access, its investigation and remediation", a useful log answers four questions about each access: who, which patient's record, what they did with it, and when. If a patient complains that a staff member shared their treatment details, the log is how you find out whether that staff member opened the record at all.

Two things follow for a small clinic:

  • Every person needs their own login. If the whole front desk shares one username and password, a log can only say "reception opened this record", which tells you nothing. A shared login also defeats the access control in 6(1)(b).
  • Paper counts too. The rule covers personal data in your possession, not only data in software. A register or file cupboard that anyone can open has no log of any kind. Locking it and keeping the key with named people is the paper version of 6(1)(b).

Your Software Vendor Does Not Carry This for You

Most clinic software is run by a company that stores your records on its servers. Under DPDP that company is your A company that handles personal data on your behalf and on your instructions, such as a software provider that stores your patient records.. Rule 6(1) puts the duty on the clinic "including in respect of any processing undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor". The clinic stays responsible even when the records sit on someone else's servers.

Rule 6(1)(f) adds that the contract between the clinic and the processor should include "appropriate provision" for these safeguards. So the questions below belong in your software agreement, not only in a sales call.

What "HIPAA-Compliant" Tells You

Not much, for an Indian clinic. HIPAA is a United States law. The US Department of Health and Human Services says its rules apply to covered entities and business associates, and that an organisation that is neither "does not have to comply". Some Indian vendors still use the term. MolarPlus's buying guide for Indian clinics, for example, says its patient data is "stored in India-based HIPAA-compliant servers".

That may describe how the servers are run, but it is not the test your clinic will be held to. The Indian rule is Rule 6. Ask the vendor to answer against Rule 6 directly.

Questions to Ask Before You Choose Software

  • Does every staff member get their own login, and can I remove a login the day someone leaves?
  • Can I limit what a reception login can see or change, compared with a dentist's login?
  • Does the software record who opened each patient's record, not just who edited it? Can I see that A running record, kept by the system, of which login opened or changed which record and when. myself?
  • How long are those logs kept? Rule 6(1)(e) asks for at least one year.
  • Are backups taken, and how would my records be restored after a failure?
  • Does your contract with me say what security safeguards you take, as Rule 6(1)(f) expects?

An honest vendor will answer some of these with "not yet". That is more useful than a badge, because it tells you exactly what you have to cover yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does DPDP require an audit trail in dental software?

DPDP Rule 6 asks for access control and for logs that give visibility on who accessed personal data, with monitoring and review. It does not use the words audit trail or require any particular product. Software that records who opened which record, and when, is one practical way to meet it.

How long must a dental clinic keep access logs under DPDP?

Rule 6(1)(e) of the DPDP Rules, 2025 says to keep such logs for one year, unless another law in force requires otherwise. The rule comes into force eighteen months after the Rules were published on 13 November 2025.

Is HIPAA-compliant software enough for DPDP?

HIPAA is a United States law and is not the test for an Indian clinic. Under DPDP, the clinic is responsible for the safeguards in Rule 6, including when a software company stores the records for it. Ask the vendor to answer against Rule 6.
