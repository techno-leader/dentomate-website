The Dental Council of India Is Gone: What the National Dental Commission Means for Your Practice

Photo: Karnanand, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Most practising dentists in India learned about the change through a news headline rather than a direct communication. On 19 March 2026, the Government of India notified that the The statutory body established by the National Dental Commission Act 2023 to replace the Dental Council of India. It regulates dental education, institutional assessment, and practitioner registration through three autonomous boards. (NDC) had come into force. Simultaneously, the Dental Council of India was dissolved and the Dentists Act 1948, which had governed the profession for nearly eight decades, was repealed.

For a practising dentist with an existing registration, the practical answer is straightforward: your licence continues, and you do not need to do anything. For a clinic owner who is about to hire a fresh graduate, the picture is more complicated. This article covers both.

What Changed on 19 March 2026

The PIB notification of 19 March 2026 confirmed three things at once: the NDC Act 2023 came into force, the Dental Council of India was dissolved with effect from the same date, and the Dentists Act 1948 stood repealed. Dr. Sanjay Tewari was appointed as the first Chairperson of the National Dental Commission.

The NDC was not created from scratch. Under Section 59 of the NDC Act 2023, the Commission is the successor in interest to the Dental Council of India. All assets and liabilities of the DCI transferred to the NDC on the same date. Functions that the DCI performed, including maintaining the Indian Dental Register, passed to the relevant NDC board.

The Three Autonomous Boards

Rather than replicating the single-body structure of the DCI, the NDC operates through three autonomous boards, each with a distinct mandate:

  • Undergraduate and Postgraduate Dental Education Board sets standards for BDS and MDS programmes, frames curriculum guidelines, and oversees the conduct of dental education.
  • Dental Assessment and Rating Board handles accreditation and institutional assessment of dental colleges, rating them against the standards the Education Board sets.
  • The NDC board that took over the DCI's registration function. State Dental Councils, which handle state-level registration, continue to exist under the NDC framework. The Ethics Board sits above them on appeals.

Your Existing Registration Is Still Valid

The question most practising dentists have is a simple one: does the dissolution of the DCI mean their licence is no longer valid? The answer, rooted in the transition provision of the NDC Act, is no.

The NDC Act 2023 provides that any person registered in the Indian Dental Register maintained under the Dentists Act 1948, prior to the NDC Act coming into force, shall be deemed to have been registered under the NDC Act and enrolled in the National Register. This is a standard succession clause. It means the register itself transferred to the Ethics and Dental Registration Board, with every entry in it intact.

No dentist who was validly registered under the DCI needs to apply again, pay a re-registration fee, or produce new documents solely because of the transition. If you have questions about a specific registration number, the correct body to contact is now the Ethics and Dental Registration Board.

This matters for clinic owners hiring fresh associates. Before a newly graduated dentist can see patients independently, they must have passed the NExT-Dental and received their registration. The transition provisions of the Act mean that graduates from batches that completed their degree before the NExT-Dental became operational are covered by the deemed-registration clause and can practice under their existing registration. For batches after that cutoff, the NExT-Dental is the requirement.

The practical check when hiring is straightforward: confirm that the candidate holds a valid registration number, whether issued by a State Dental Council or the NDC, before they start seeing patients independently.

What Happened to the DCI Website and Records

The dciindia.gov.in domain has been retained and, at the time of writing, still carries information from the DCI era alongside NDC transition notices. Because the NDC is the legal successor in interest to the DCI, all registration records, disciplinary records, and the Indian Dental Register itself transferred to the NDC. The Ethics and Dental Registration Board is the body that now holds and maintains those records.

If you need an official copy of your registration certificate or a verification letter, the correct point of contact has shifted from the DCI to the Ethics and Dental Registration Board. The PMC review article on this transition, "A New Era in Dental Regulation", notes that dental colleges and practitioners should expect updated operational guidelines as the NDC frames its regulations under the Act.

What the NDC Does Not Change

For a clinic running day-to-day, several things are unaffected by the transition. Your GST treatment for dental services continues to be governed by Notification 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), which has nothing to do with the DCI or the NDC. Your obligation to retain clinical records, your DPDP compliance responsibilities, and your NABH accreditation status, if any, all sit under their own regulatory regimes. The NDC governs who may practice dentistry and how dental education is conducted; it does not supersede the other compliance obligations that run alongside it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my DCI registration still valid after the NDC took over?

Yes. The NDC Act 2023 provides that any person registered in the Indian Dental Register under the Dentists Act 1948 before the Act came into force shall be deemed to have been registered under the NDC Act and enrolled in the National Register. No action is required on your part.

Do I need to re-register with the National Dental Commission?

No. Existing registrations transfer automatically under the deemed-registration provision of the NDC Act. You do not need to apply again or pay a fresh registration fee because of the transition.

What does NExT-Dental mean for hiring a fresh BDS graduate?

Section 15 of the NDC Act 2023 requires new BDS graduates to pass the National Exit Test (Dental) before they can practice independently or enrol in a postgraduate programme. Before you take on a fresh associate, confirm whether their graduating batch was covered by the NExT-Dental requirement or falls under a transition provision.

What happened to the DCI's assets, staff, and functions?

Section 59 of the NDC Act 2023 makes the NDC the successor in interest to the Dental Council of India. All assets and liabilities of the DCI transferred to the NDC on 19 March 2026.
