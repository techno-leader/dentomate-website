Should I Get WhatsApp API for My Clinic?

Photo: Yuri Samoilov (yuri.samoilov.online), CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

If you run a clinic in India, someone has probably offered to "get you the official WhatsApp API with a green tick". The pitch sounds like an upgrade from the WhatsApp Business app on the reception phone. Sometimes it is. For most single-chair and two-chair practices it is a set of new obligations that someone at the clinic now has to look after.

This article walks through what owning that account actually involves, using Meta's own documentation, so you can decide before you pay. If you have already decided, our step-by-step registration guide covers how to do it.

What You Would Be Signing Up For

"WhatsApp API" means the WhatsApp Business Platform. There are two ways to get onto it, and neither gives you something your receptionist can open and type into on day one.

Going directly to Meta. Meta's Cloud API get-started guide lists the steps: register as a Meta developer, create a Meta app, generate access tokens, and set up a An internet address your own software runs, where Meta delivers each incoming patient message and each delivery receipt. If nothing is listening there, replies go nowhere you can see. endpoint. Every one of those is a developer's job. The Cloud API is an interface for software; somebody still has to build or rent the screen where replies get read and written.

Going through a provider. The other route is a A company Meta works with to connect businesses to the WhatsApp Business Platform. It supplies the inbox and campaign tools, and sets its own prices on top of Meta's., which supplies the inbox and handles the developer steps. This is also the only route if you want to keep the number you already use. Meta's guide to onboarding WhatsApp Business app users describes connecting an existing Business app number to the Platform through a partner's Embedded Signup flow. So "keep your number" still means a provider.

The Bills Come From Two Places

Meta charges per delivered message. Its pricing page links the India rate card, which on 9 October 2026 listed ₹0.8631 for a marketing message and ₹0.115 each for utility, authentication and service messages. Each business phone number gets 1,000 free service messages a month, and the free tier does not roll over. The same page says Meta may change prices on the first day of any quarter, so up to four times a year.

Owning the account also means owning two deadlines from that page:

  • A payment method. Without one on the account, Meta delivers service messages within the free tier but not after it. Replies to patients simply stop once the 1,000 are used.
  • Rupee billing. Businesses billed directly by Meta with India as their billing country must move every Messaging account to INR by 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027, Meta "will no longer deliver the messages" of eligible accounts that have not moved.

A provider then adds its own margin. AiSensy is one example. On 9 October 2026 its pricing page listed ₹1.09 for a marketing message and ₹0.145 for utility, authentication and service, against Meta's ₹0.8631 and ₹0.115. That is about 26% more per message, before the plan fee, and its add-ons are priced separately and marked "+ GST". Other providers price differently; ask for the per-message rate in writing.

To put the per-message part in perspective: a recall to 300 lapsed patients is 300 marketing messages. At Meta's rate that is about ₹259. At the AiSensy rate above it is ₹327. The message charges are rarely what makes owning an account expensive. The plan fee and the hours are.

A New Account Starts at 250 Patients a Day

Meta's messaging limits page says a newly created business portfolio can message 250 unique people outside a The 24 hours after a patient last messaged you. Inside it you can reply freely. Outside it, you can only reach them with a template Meta has approved in advance. in any moving 24-hour period. That limit rises to 2,000 once you verify your business, or once you have delivered 2,000 high-quality template messages to unique people within 30 days. After that it grows automatically, but only if you have used at least half of your current limit over the previous seven days.

For a clinic sending a recall to 600 patients who have not visited in a year, a new account means splitting it across at least three days. That is manageable, but it is one more thing to plan around.

A second limit matters more for recall. Meta's page on per-user marketing limits says WhatsApp may limit how many marketing messages a person receives from any business when they are "less likely to be receptive", judged partly on how many marketing messages they have been reading. Those sends fail with error 131049. A list of patients who have not heard from the clinic in a year is exactly the audience this affects, so a recall blast from a brand-new account will not reach everyone on the list.

About the "Green Tick"

This is usually the selling point, so it is worth reading what Meta says. Its Official Business Accounts page says an approved number shows "a blue checkmark beside their name in the contacts view". Meta's page does not describe a green tick at all. To be eligible, all of these must be true:

  • The business follows the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy.
  • It has been registered on the WhatsApp Business Platform for at least 30 days.
  • Its business portfolio has passed business verification.
  • The number has two-step verification turned on.
  • Its display name has been approved.

Meta says it does not grant the status to WhatsApp Business app numbers, and that a rejected request can only be resubmitted after 30 days. So a "green tick package" is a Platform account plus a request that Meta can turn down. Nobody selling the package can promise the outcome, and Meta's page says nothing about the checkmark improving delivery.

Who Should Get Their Own Account

None of this makes your own account wrong. It suits a clinic that has some of these:

  • A multi-branch group with an IT person or developer, or a budget to pay a provider's plan every month.
  • A firm requirement that every message comes from the clinic's own number and name.
  • Its own software that needs to send and receive WhatsApp messages directly.
  • Someone who will watch the payment method, the INR migration, template approvals and the messaging limit as part of their job.

If that describes you, the registration guide walks through the five steps. If it does not, the question to ask is whether you need an account at all, or only the messages.

Questions to Ask Before You Pay Anyone

  • Is the account registered in my clinic's business portfolio, or in yours?
  • What is your per-message rate for each category, against Meta's rate card on the same day?
  • What is the monthly plan fee, and is GST included?
  • Who adds the payment method, and who handles the INR migration before 31 December 2026?
  • If you promise a tick, what happens to my money if Meta refuses it?
  • Can I take the number and the account with me if I leave?

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need my own WhatsApp API to send appointment reminders?

No. Reminders need a WhatsApp Business Platform account somewhere, but it does not have to be yours. Clinic software that already holds an account can send them for you. You need your own only if the messages must come from your clinic's number.

Is the green tick on WhatsApp worth paying for?

Meta's current page describes a blue checkmark, not a green tick, and grants it only to Platform numbers that meet its criteria, after at least 30 days on the Platform. Meta can refuse the request. No package can promise it, and Meta's page does not say it improves delivery.

Can I keep my existing WhatsApp Business number if I move to the API?

Meta describes a way to connect an existing WhatsApp Business app number to the Platform, but it runs through a partner's Embedded Signup flow. In practice that means going through a Business Solution Provider.
